# Our Team

## Director

*Melinda Benson, Regents' Professor, Geography and Environmental Studies  *

<img src="{{ '/about/morgan_24.jpg' | relative_url }}" class="img-left" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="284" height="427" alt="Melinda Morgan Headshot" />Melinda Morgan is a professor in Geography and Environmental Studies at The University of New Mexico. Along with University of Utah law professor Robin Craig, she is the author of [The End of Sustainability: Resilience and the Future of Environmental Governance in the Anthropocene](https://kansaspress.ku.edu/9780700625161/). Her research and scholarship focus on the emerging environmental governance regimes, with a particular focus on how these approaches to environmental challenges interface with existing legal and regulatory requirements. She teaches courses in natural resource management, including public lands, water governance, and introduction to environmental studies. You can find links to her papers available on her [Social Science Research Network](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/cf_dev/AbsByAuth.cfm?per_id=1225638) page.

## **Associate Director**

*Dr. Fred Gibbs, Associate Professor, Department of History *

*<img src="{{ '/about/gibbs-photo.jpg' | relative_url }}" class="img-left" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="288" height="282" alt="Fred Gibbs Headshot" />*

Fred Gibbs is Associate Professor of History and directs [Amaranth](https://amaranth.unm.edu/), UNM's digital humanities and public scholarship studio. His current history of medicine research traces the long history of "natural" diets, and his digital humanities work spans historical GIS, digital storytelling, and historical epistemology, with a focus on AI-assisted scholarship. He teaches courses at the intersection of history, health, expertise, technology, and nature — all of which ask how ideas dietary expertise, health, and food production, get built, contested, and mapped onto particular landscapes.

## Graduate Assistant

*Desiree Loggins, Geography and Environmental Studies Ph.D. student *  
<img src="{{ '/about/desiree-photo.jpeg' | relative_url }}" class="img-left" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="356" height="267" alt="Desiree Loggins Headshot " />Desiree is a second year Ph.D. student in the Department of Geography and Environmental Studies at UNM. Her research centers place-based communities and their relationships to land, water, and modern environmental management in New Mexico. Her dissertation will focus on the political ecology of gender and technology in acequia governance and acequia lifeways. Prior to academia, Desiree worked at the National Audubon Society as a facilitator, organizer, and advocate for flowing rivers, working lands, and resilient communities in the arid West. She has an undergraduate degree in Environmental Studies from University of California, Santa Cruz, and a Masters of Science in Geography and Environmental Studies. Read her latest essay on the afterlife of colonial water infrastructure in the anthology, [Water Bodies: Love Letters to the Most  Abundant Substance on Earth.](https://www.torreyhouse.org/water-bodies) Desiree is also collaborating with [CESOS](https://www.cesoss.org/) on community engaged work as a Center fellow.
