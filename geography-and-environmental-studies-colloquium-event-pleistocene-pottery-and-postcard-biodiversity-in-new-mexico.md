Pleistocene, Pottery, and Postcard: BIOdiversity in New Mexico

Pleistocene, Pottery, Postcard will serve as three different markers of time: 20,000+ years ago; 1000+ years ago; and the present day. Seen through the lens of environmental visual culture and environmental humanities, these markers collectively help us to apprehend not only the diversity of nonhuman life but also the diversity of human-nonhuman relations across a vast span of time in what we call today New Mexico.

Even though New Mexico ranks among the top five states in the US in biodiversity and also diversity of human cultures, rarely do we honor and celebrate this biocultural vitality. I’ll speak about the exhibition and book project provisionally titled: Coexistence: Biodiversity in New Mexico that I’m co-curating with Dr. Josie Lopez, Head Curator and Curator of Art at the Albuquerque Museum. I’ll discuss how the theme of “coexistence” matters not only for New Mexico but is essential for shaping a more just global biodiversity conservation framework.
